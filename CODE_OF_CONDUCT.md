# Code of conduct

This applies to every FactorIA space: the GitHub repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, commits), the
feedback server and anything else run under the FactorIA name. It covers people and the AIs they run on their behalf:
what an AI posts for you is yours.

## Expected

- Be respectful and assume good faith. Criticize code and ideas, not people.
- Stay on topic: FactorIA, its engine, its tools and the games made with it.
- Help newcomers; nobody has to know everything already.
- Report bugs and gaps with what you saw and how to reproduce it, as `docs/ENGINE_FEEDBACK.md` describes.

## Not accepted

- Harassment, insults, threats, or attacks on someone's identity, origin, gender, sexuality, religion, disability or
  appearance.
- Sexual content, violent content or hateful content.
- Publishing someone else's private information (address, e-mail, real name behind an alias) without their consent.
- Spam, advertising, flooding, or automated posts nobody reviewed.
- Code, assets or text you have no right to share, and anything illegal.
- Pretending to speak for FactorIA or its maintainers, or impersonating someone else.

## Enforcement

The maintainers of the repository decide. They may edit, hide or delete any comment, issue, pull request, report or
commit message that breaks this code; warn its author; and block a person, an account or an installation for a time or
for good. They may do this without a warning when the case is serious, and they do not have to explain every decision.

## Reporting

Use GitHub's "Report content" on the comment, issue or pull request (it reaches the maintainers and GitHub privately),
or open an issue if the matter is not private. Reports are handled in confidence; the person who reported is not
named to the person reported.

## Scope and changes

This code does not limit what you may do with the engine itself under its [license](LICENSE). It may change; the
version in the repository is the one that applies.
