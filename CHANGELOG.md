## Unreleased

- **Stale shader twins and caches (M73):** the mesh shader template changed, and its hash is stamped in every material twin (`<material>.json.<dialect>.shader`) and in the shader cache (`.factoria/shader-cache`): the twins of a game exported with an earlier version and the cached shaders no longer match the engine. The cache builds itself again on the next run; export the game again to get new twins (a mobile or web game, whose player has no shader compiler, needs it to run materials again).
- **Render polish (M73):** a glass or transmissive material that is `unlit` (the mesh flag or `lighting: "unlit"`) or a full shader no longer takes a transmission copy it never reads, and the cost panel and the subsurface pass use one rule for which meshes scatter; a transmission pipeline that cannot be made is reported once and draws without the glass instead of drawing with an invalid pipeline; on Metal the frame's first transient write no longer lets a read-back skip a commit; `render.probes` without `entity` ends the watch of a mesh; the box side and the blend distance of a `ReflectionProbe` are held in the ranges their doc strings give before they reach the renderer; a probe manifest with a `page` that leaves the project folder, a malformed `hash` or a huge `levels` is refused with the JSON pointer and the fix; `probe.bake` gives two probe ids that differ only in characters a file name cannot hold two different manifests (`a_b-<hash>`); a NaN light brightness or range ranks last in the local shadow budget instead of unordering the ranking.

- **Reflection probes are read by the meshes (M73-P3c):** a lit mesh inside the box of a `ReflectionProbe` now reflects it: up to two probes per mesh (the larger `importance`, then the smaller box, then the nearer capture) plus the sky, blended by how deep the mesh is inside each box over `blendDistance` (weights in steps of 1/32, so meshes that reflect the same still share a draw), with the roughness level of the filtered map, box projection when `boxProjection` is on, and the probe's spherical harmonics as the diffuse ambient where no lightmap or light probe covers the surface. The sky is entry 0 of the same atlas, which replaces the sky's cube at the mesh fragment view 14 (no new texture unit; a scene with a sky material and no probe now keeps the 8 MB atlas too). A scene with no probe in reach draws the same pixels and the same draw calls as before. `render.supports("reflectionProbes")` is true wherever the renderer draws, `render.probes` takes an `entity` and tells which probes that mesh reflected and how much of each, and the editor draws the box of the selected probe. A probe is read from the frame after its capture is complete; the captures themselves see the sky only (no bounce). Docs: docs/rendering/RENDERING_PROBES.md and docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_PROBES.md ("What P5d-2 can use").
- **Realtime GI, the shading (M73-P5d-2):** lit meshes now read a ready `RealtimeGI` grid: the diffuse ambient light of a pixel is the light of the eight
  probes around it, weighted by distance, by whether the probe faces the surface and by a Chebyshev visibility test on the probe's depth map (a thin wall keeps
  the light of one side from the other), times `intensity`. The order of the diffuse light is the lightmap texel, the baked light probes over their coverage (the rest of that pixel is the ambient light, not the grid), the
  grid (fading out over half a spacing outside its probes, so the box must reach past the surfaces it lights), then the reflection probes' light and the sky;
  the specular light is unchanged. `bounce` works: a capture multiplies the grid's light by it, so the next refresh of a probe sees one more bounce (0 = the
  captures ignore the grid), while the camera sees it at full strength. A grid that is still filling changes nothing, a world without a `RealtimeGI` draws the
  same frames as before, and no draw call or texture unit is added (the records are in the light data texture, the fragment block grew by one vec4).
  `render.gi_grid` reports `meshesRead` per grid and `render.supports` lists `realtimeGI` as drawn. Every material twin was regenerated.
  docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHT_PROBES.md and docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_PROBES.md, "Realtime GI: the shading".
- **Realtime GI, the data side (M73-P5d-1):** a new `RealtimeGI` component (a box of probes, `spacing`, `probesPerFrame`, `resolution`, `hysteresis`,
  `nearExclusion`, `maxDistance`, `backfaceTolerance`, tags; a wrong field is reported with its JSON pointer and the fix) makes the renderer capture a grid of
  probes over a few frames with the reflection probes' six-face capture: each probe keeps the diffuse light that reaches it as nine spherical-harmonics
  coefficients and a small octahedral map of the mean and mean squared distance of the surfaces around it (as fractions of `maxDistance`, so they fit a half float), blended into its old value by `hysteresis`, in a
  band of 132 rows added to the probe atlas (no texture unit). The probes are refreshed in a fixed order, the longest ago first, within `probesPerFrame` (32
  for all grids), and everything stays on the GPU. New command `render.gi_grid` lists each grid's probe count, progress, memory and the probes inside geometry.
  A scene without a `RealtimeGI` costs nothing. docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHT_PROBES.md and docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_PROBES.md.
- **Realtime GI and probe fixes (M73-P5d-2b):** a reflection probe whose tile was emptied (a RealtimeGI made the atlas taller, the tile moved or was evicted) is no longer sampled
  until it is captured again, so meshes stop reflecting black for a few frames. A `RealtimeGI` grid keeps its captured probes when its entity moves less than half a spacing (a larger move or other
  `showTags` / `hideTags` lays it out again), and a failed capture stays visible in `render.gi_grid` until the next one succeeds. The atlas no longer grows by 132 rows on a GPU without RGBA16F blending,
  `render.probes {entity}` keeps the picks of that one mesh only, and a selected `RealtimeGI` draws its probe box and probe points in the editor's perspective viewport.
- **Glass and liquids (M73.6b):** `MeshRenderer.transmission` (0..1), `thickness` (metres), `attenuationColor` and `attenuationDistance` now draw: the mesh
  shows what is behind it, refracted by its `ior` and `thickness`, blurred by its `roughness`, tinted by `color` and by the Beer-Lambert attenuation of its
  volume. glTF models bring them (`KHR_materials_transmission`, `_volume`, `_ior`; the textures are not used and the import says so). When a visible draw is
  transmissive the main pass is split once per camera: the opaque picture is copied into a refraction atlas (the camera's rectangle and four half-size
  reductions, in the colour format of the picture, so HDR values refract unclamped, the filtering done in the shader) and the glass is drawn with the
  blended meshes, far to near, back faces culled unless `doubleSided`; a frame with no visible transmissive draw copies nothing and is bit for bit what it
  was. Glass does not see glass, opaque objects in front can leak into the refraction, and full shaders and custom lighting get none (documented). If the
  copy cannot be made the glass is blended plainly (`game.render_stats` `transmissionPlain`; `render.supports("transmission")` is true). New command
  `render.material_cost {id}` and a "Render cost" block under the MeshRenderer fields of the Inspector say what a material costs (extra passes, phase,
  shader-only features) from one source in the renderer. `game.render_stats` reports `transmissiveMeshes` and `transmissionCopies`. Every material twin was
  regenerated (the mesh template changed). See docs/rendering/RENDERING_TRANSMISSION.md and docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_SCREEN_EFFECTS.md.
- **Metal: a view could draw with another frame's data when the GPU was behind (fix):** on macOS and iOS the per-frame data the CPU writes for the GPU (the instances of the meshes, the light data, the 2D stream, video frames) could be overwritten while the GPU of the frame before the last still read it, so an extra view whose content changes from frame to frame (a CameraView, a render-target camera, a time-sliced probe capture) could show a wrong frame's meshes on a loaded GPU. The renderer now waits for that frame before it writes, the wait sokol made anyway a moment later.
- **Reflection probes, capture and atlas (M73-P3b):** a `ReflectionProbe` entity now captures the light around it: six views of the scene, filtered into a mirror picture, two to four rougher ones (by resolution) and nine spherical-harmonics coefficients, kept in one 1024 x 1024 RGBA16F atlas (RGBA8 where the GPU cannot render to half floats) that also holds the sky as entry 0. Realtime probes capture a few cube faces a frame (`timeSliced`, the default, `everyFrame` or `onDemand`; two faces a frame in all by default, so the cost is a number and not the count of probes); a full atlas evicts only a probe of strictly lower `importance`. The new `probe.bake` command captures a probe at once and writes a Radiance page plus a `.probe.json` manifest next to the scene (the same scene gives the same bytes; the renderer reads a rebake without restarting), the new `render.probes` command lists the atlas, the sky's entry and every probe with its state and tile, and the new `render.probe_refresh` command captures a realtime probe again (what an `onDemand` probe waits for). A scene that is reloaded or replaced makes every realtime probe capture the new scene. The meshes read the atlas from M73-P3c (the entry above); a frame without probes costs nothing. Docs: docs/rendering/RENDERING_PROBES.md and docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_PROBES.md.
- **Baking polish (M73):** the bake memory estimate counts 320 bytes per triangle, the automatic `rayEpsilon` is at most 1 cm, the next bake deletes the pages and `.probes` files a previous one left behind, `lightmap.bake` is refused while the game runs (`game_running`), the job registry keeps the last 8 finished jobs, `render.light_probes` reports `stale` per group and `unbaked` scene groups (the Lightmap window shows them), the probe gizmo skips probes behind the camera and **View > Show light probes** hides them, probe light is culled by group box, and the Radiance decoder allocates once, after checking the file, with a size limit.
- **Subsurface scattering (M73.5):** `MeshRenderer.subsurface` (strength 0..1), `subsurfaceRadius` (metres, 0..0.5, the distance that holds 90% of the
  light) and `subsurfaceColor` (the spread of each colour channel) make skin, wax and marble: the diffuse light of the mesh is drawn into an image of its
  own, blurred along x and y with a depth-aware separable profile of our own (four Gaussians fitted to the diffusion profile of skin, the red tail wider
  than the blue) and the change is added to the picture. Only the diffuse light is blurred, never the specular light, the sky reflection or the emission;
  the light never crosses a silhouette or reaches another object. It works with MSAA (the sss images themselves are single-sample), render scale, split
  screen and HDR, and a scene with no `subsurface` is bit for bit what it was. Opaque meshes of the engine's own shader scatter (not a mesh with `material`,
  an unlit or a transparent one). Without RGBA16F targets the meshes draw without scattering and `render.supports("subsurface")` says so;
  `game.render_stats` reports `subsurfaceCameras` and `subsurfaceDraws`. Every material twin was regenerated (the mesh template changed). See
  docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md, "Subsurface scattering", and docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_SUBSURFACE.md.
- **Light probes (M73.4d):** `LightProbeGroup` probes are baked by `lightmap.bake`; a mesh without a lightmap gets their light, a `baked` light no longer lights it twice. docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHT_PROBES.md.
- **Lightmap bake (M73.4c):** `lightmap.bake` (waits), `lightmap.bake_start`, `lightmap.bake_status` and `lightmap.bake_cancel` bake the open scene's `baked` and `mixed` lights, its sky (the environment map, the gradient or the ambient light) and its emissive and lit meshes into Radiance `.hdr` pages and a `.lightmap.json` manifest next to the scene, unwrapping the meshes that have no second UV set, and the renderer reads them at once (`SceneSettings.lightmaps` is set in memory: keep it with `scene.save` in a batch, or write it into the scene). The job reports a phase and progress, can be cancelled in every phase, writes the pages and then the manifest atomically (a file open on Windows gives an error that says what to close) and gives the same bytes with any number of threads. The editor has **View > Lightmap** (options, progress bar, Cancel, a summary of the pages, one undo step for the setting). The baker core now checks its input for NaN and infinity, derives its default ray offset from the texel size (not 10 cm in a kilometer-wide scene) and checks for cancel in the BVH build, the rasterizer, the denoiser and the gutter fill. Disc and Tube area lights are baked as rectangles. Docs: docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md, "Baking".
- **Contact shadows (M73.2c):** `SceneSettings.contactShadows` and a new `Light.contactShadowLength` (metres, 0 = off, the default) add a
  short screen-space ray from each lit point toward the light, marched in the mesh shader against the depth prepass (12 steps, a thickness
  test, a per-pixel noise that does not change from frame to frame, a fade at the end of the ray, at the screen's edge and from 20 to 40 m
  from the camera). It multiplies into the light's own shadow term, so the sun, point, spot and area lights take it with or without a
  shadow map, and a custom `lighting()` gets it through `fa_light()` / `fa_light_shadow()`. It sees only what the camera sees (no caster
  off screen, thin objects let the light through); blended meshes, full-shader materials and meshes with `receiveShadows` off have none.
  Turning it on draws the prepass once; with it off the frame is bit for bit what it was. `render.supports("contactShadows")` follows the
  depth prepass; `game.render_stats` reports `contactShadowLights`. See docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md, "Contact shadows".
- **Volumetric fog and light shafts (M73.5):** `SceneSettings.volumetricFog` fills the view with a medium (`volumetricDensity`,
  `volumetricDistance`, `volumetricHeightBase` / `volumetricHeightFalloff`, `volumetricAnisotropy`, `volumetricAmbient`, colour `fogColor`) that the
  ambient light, the sun and the point and spot lights with `Light.volumetric` above 0 shine through; the shadows of the shadow atlas cut the
  shafts. While a camera draws it, its analytic fog (`fogDensity`, the `PostProcess` fog) is off, so nothing is fogged twice; transparent meshes are
  fogged like the surface behind them. A half or quarter resolution raymarch with a depth-aware upsample, on every backend with no compute shader;
  `volumetricQuality` (`low` for phones and the web, `medium`, `high`) bounds the cost, and the jitter depends on the pixel only (no temporal
  reprojection). A scene without `volumetricFog` is unchanged and compiles nothing. `render.supports("volumetricFog")` is true wherever the GPU has
  RGBA16F render targets that blend and filter; `game.render_stats` reports `volumetricCameras`, `volumetricLights` and `volumetricLightsDropped`.
- **Sky ambient light from spherical harmonics (M73-P3e):** the diffuse ambient light of every mesh under a sky material, a physical
  sky or an HDRI now comes from nine RGB coefficients (L2 spherical harmonics) that the renderer projects, in one GPU pass, into a
  4x4 mip of the sky cube it already bakes, instead of from the 32x32 irradiance cube, which is gone (view 15 is free; it is reserved
  for the screen texture). Same re-bake keys, same cost, deterministic, on GL 4.1, GLES3, WebGL2, D3D11 and Metal (an RGBA8
  fallback where the GPU cannot render to half floats). A custom shader's `ambient()` still gets `ambientColor`, the whole ambient term;
  see docs/shaders/SHADERS_LIGHTING.md. A sun in an HDRI now smears into the ambient light a little (the golden `3d_hdri_spheres`
  changed, mean 0.0027); the physical-sky goldens moved by one level at most.
- **Environment from an HDRI (M73-P3a-2):** `SceneSettings.environmentMap` now works. It is an equirectangular Radiance `.hdr`
  (`assets.import` takes it into `assets/hdri/`, kind `hdri`; a cube cross, a strip of faces or a light probe is refused with the layout
  named and how to convert it; `project.validate` checks the file a scene names) that lights every mesh (ambient light and
  reflections, through the same prefiltered environment the sky uses: one bake, redone only when the map, `environmentRotation`,
  `environmentIntensity` or the file change) and is drawn as the background (new `environmentBackground`, true by default). A
  `skyMaterial` still wins the background; the lighting comes from the map when it is set, else from the sky. Pictures wider than
  4096 are shrunk for the GPU; NaN and negative texels become 0, a sun above 60000 is scaled down with its colour kept, and the
  blurred levels limit one sample to 64, so a sun leaves no fireflies. Scenes without an `environmentMap` are pixel-identical.
  `render.supports("environmentMap")` is true where the GPU renders to RGBA16F.
- **Area lights, light cookies and IES profiles (M73.1b):** `Light.type = "area"` with `shape` (`rectangle`, `disc`, `tube`) and `size` lights with Linearly Transformed Cosines, diffuse and specular, with the energy of the point light it replaces at a distance, and the clusters see its real extent; `Light.cookie` (a texture) projects through a spot light (perspective) or a directional one (tiled, `size` is its world size); `Light.ies` (an `.ies` LM-63 file, read by our own parser, normalised by its peak) shapes a point or spot light. The LTC tables, the cookies and the profiles live in one RGBA16F light atlas (no new texture slot). `assets.import` takes `.ies` into `assets/lights/`, and `render.supports areaLights`, `lightCookies` and `iesProfiles` are real (they need `half`). A scene without them is pixel-identical and keeps its cost; a point light's cookie is an equirectangular picture stored as a cube of six faces in the atlas (picked by the same face selection as its shadow). Area lights cast shadows through the local shadow tiles of M73.2b (six faces from the emitter's centre, a PCSS penumbra as wide as the emitter), materials with layers get the LTC integral for their base layer, and `lighting()` / `light()` hooks and full shaders get the light of the representative point of the emitter instead of its centre (the 2D light stays a point). The light stride is 8 texels when a light has an area, a cookie or a profile (`fa_ambient.w`). Docs: docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md, docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_RENDERING_LIGHTING.md, "The light atlas".
- **Shadows of point and spot lights (M73.2b):** a `Light` of type `spot` with `castShadows` has one shadow map and a `point` light six faces, in the same shadow atlas as the sun's cascades. A deterministic budget per frame ranks the casting lights by brightness, range and distance to the camera, sizes each map from `shadowResolution` (0 = from the distance), halves the maps and then drops the least important light whole when the atlas (`SceneSettings.shadowAtlasSize`) or the 64 maps of a frame are full, and warns once with the fix; `Light.shadowDistance` fades a local light's shadow out and limits it. The sun's cascades keep their size until every local map is at its minimum and are dropped last; a baked light gets a map only for the meshes with no lightmap near it. PCF, hard and PCSS work as for the sun with the same code; `render.supports pointShadows` / `spotShadows` follow `float32Targets`. Shadow draws count in `drawCalls` and `shadowCasters`. A scene with no local caster is drawn exactly as before; the custom-shader twin files are regenerated (the light data texture grew a shadow region, documented in docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_RENDERING_LIGHTING.md).
- **Physical sky (M73-P3d):** `SceneSettings.skyModel = "physical"` draws a sky computed from the sun (the light with `castShadows`, else the brightest directional light), the air (Rayleigh, Mie, ozone, multiple scattering) and procedural clouds that drift with the game clock, with a sun disc, and bakes it into the image-based lighting so reflections and ambient light follow it; the sun light is tinted by the air and the clouds (`skyTintsSun`). New settings `skySunSize`, `skyTurbidity`, `skyGroundAlbedo`, `skyExposure`, `skyTintsSun`, `cloudCoverage`, `cloudDensity`, `cloudAltitude`, `cloudWind`; `render.supports physicalSky` is real (it needs RGBA16F render targets). The environment is rebuilt in three slices over three frames while the sun or the clouds move slowly. Docs: docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md, "The physical sky".
- **Cascaded shadow maps (M73.2a):** the sun's shadow is cut into `SceneSettings.shadowCascades` (1..4) cascades fitted to the camera, stable (no shimmer while the camera moves), blended at the seams (`shadowCascadeBlend`, `shadowCascadeSplit`) and filtered by PCF, hard or PCSS (`Light.shadowFilter`, `shadowSoftness`); `shadowResolution`, `shadowBias`, `shadowNormalBias`, `shadowDistance` and `shadowAtlasSize` now work, in one shadow atlas of 32-bit float that has room for the point and spot maps of M73.2b. `render.supports cascadedShadows` follows `float32Targets`; `render.debug_view cascades` tints the surfaces by cascade. The shadow of a scene with `castShadows` changes a little (a sharper, stable edge); the custom-shader twin files are regenerated (`fa_shadow_coord` is now the world position).
- **`factoria feedback collect examples/*`** no longer reports "no 'entries' array" for the files of that folder that are not feedback files (`examples.json`, a readme): when several paths are given, a file not called `ENGINE_FEEDBACK.json` that holds no feedback is skipped; named alone it is an error that says what to pass. A file skipped because it is not valid JSON is named in the report's new `notes` list.
- **`factoria validate`** warns when the snippet of a sprite material without `buffers` reads `fa_instance_index` (it is always 0 there); comments and string literals do not count as a use.
- **Graph panel:** a file rewritten with the same time and size is found when the poll before it saw the file less than a second old (the panel polls every 20 frames and drops the content hash once a poll finds the file older than a second).
- **Lightmap baker core (M73.4, no command yet):** `fa/scene/lightmap_bake.h` bakes the diffuse light of lights, a sky and emissive surfaces into lightmap atlases and into spherical-harmonics probes with a deterministic CPU path tracer (the same bits with any number of threads, a public `TriBvh`, progress, cancel and refusals before it allocates); see docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_BAKING.md.
- **Lightmap runtime and lightmap UVs (M73.4a):** `SceneSettings.lightmaps` names a `*.lightmap.json` manifest (entity id, mesh, page, rectangle) with Radiance `.hdr` pages; the renderer loads it with the scene, hot-reloads it and reports a bad manifest, a missing page or a mesh that changed since the bake (`render.assets` failures `lightmap`, `lightmap-page`, `lightmap-stale`) while drawing the scene without it. A lightmapped entity (`receiveGI`, `lightmapScale` above 0) takes its baked light instead of the ambient and sky diffuse light (view 26 `fa_lightmap`, specular IBL stays), and `baked` lights stop lighting it; entities without an entry render exactly as before. Lightmap UVs are glTF `TEXCOORD_1` or a deterministic generated unwrap kept in memory, primitives included (`fa/render/lightmap_geometry.h`); a custom mesh material that lists `uv1` in `vertexAttributes` receives the lightmap UV there. `render.supports` answers `lightmaps`. See docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md, "Lightmaps".
- **Radiance HDR reader and writer (M73.3a, P3a-1):** `fa/core/image_float.h` adds `ImageF`, a float32 RGB or RGBA image (bilinear
  sampling with clamp or wrap, box resize, sRGB conversion with exposure to and from `Image`) and the engine's own `.hdr`
  codec: `decode_hdr` / `load_hdr` read run-length, flat and old-style rows and refuse other orientations, XYZE and
  oversized headers with an error that says what to do; `encode_hdr` / `save_hdr` write run-length rows. Not reachable from
  scripts or `assets.import` yet; the HDRI environment import builds on it. New fuzz target `hdr`.
### M73: 3D lighting of a leading engine

- **Clustered forward lighting (M73.1a):** a view is lit by up to 1024 lights at once (32 directional) instead of 32, on every
  backend (OpenGL, GLES3, WebGL2, D3D11, Metal). The view is cut into tiles of 64 pixels by 24 depth slices, the CPU builds the light
  list of each cluster as a deterministic job, and each fragment reads only its cluster's lights from one RGBA32F data texture. A scene
  with up to 32 lights is pixel-identical. `Light.innerAngle` now works. `game.render_stats` (and `renderStats()`) report
  `lightsCulled`, `lightClusters`, `lightClusterEntries`, `lightClusterClipped` and `lightMaxPerCluster`; the limits are 128 lights per
  cluster and 524288 list entries per view. In a snippet material `fa_light_count()` / `fa_light(n, i)` give the lights of the
  fragment's cluster; a full shader keeps the first 32 of the table and has `fa_cluster_light_count()` / `fa_cluster_light_index()`.
  `render.supports` answers `true` for `clusteredLights`. docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md, "Many lights".
- **Projected decals (M73.5b):** a `Decal` component paints an albedo (and tint, opacity and a normal map) on the meshes inside its box,
  projected along its local -Z, lit and shadowed like the surface under it; `angleFade`, `sortOrder` and `layers` control it, and
  `MeshRenderer.receiveDecals` / `decalLayer` choose which meshes take which decals. The decal boxes are binned into the same cluster
  grid as the lights, their textures share one atlas, and the mesh shader paints them once before the lights (`fa_apply_decals`; a
  full shader calls it itself), on every backend. Scenes without decals are pixel-identical. Limits (counts): 256 decals per view, 32 per
  cluster, 131072 list entries, 512 textures. `game.render_stats` reports `decals`, `decalsDropped`, `decalClusterClipped`;
  `render.supports` answers `true` for `decals`. docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md, "Decals".
- **The base of M73 (P0), no visible change:** the mesh shaders' lights and shadow lookup moved into three shared files
  (`light_data.glsl`, `light_loop.glsl`, `shadow_common.glsl`; the pictures are pixel-identical and the twin fixtures of the
  custom materials were regenerated); the texture slots of the mesh fragment shader are a table with static asserts (at most 16
  units, the material keeps views 6..9; docs/design/ARCHITECTURE_RENDERING_LIGHTING.md); and the fields the next steps read exist
  already, each saying "No effect yet: M73.x brings it": `Light` (`type: "area"`, `innerAngle`, `shape`, `size`, `cookie`, `ies`,
  `shadowResolution`, `shadowBias`, `shadowNormalBias`, `shadowFilter`, `shadowSoftness`, `shadowDistance`, `mode`, `volumetric`),
  `SceneSettings` (`shadowCascades`, `shadowCascadeSplit`, `shadowCascadeBlend`, `shadowDistance`, `shadowAtlasSize`,
  `contactShadows`, `environmentMap`, `environmentIntensity`, `environmentRotation`, `skyModel`) and `MeshRenderer` (`lightmapScale`,
  `contributeGI`, `receiveGI`, `clearcoat`, `clearcoatRoughness`, `sheenColor`, `sheenRoughness`, `anisotropy`,
  `anisotropyRotation`, `specular`, `ior`, `transmission`, `thickness`, `attenuationColor`, `attenuationDistance`, `subsurface`).
  The defaults keep today's look and are not written to a scene file, so scenes made before load and save as they did; an area
  light shines like a point light until M73.1b. `render.supports` lists 21 lighting features, all `planned (M73)` until their step
  lands, and the capability `float32Targets` (R32F and RGBA32F render and sample). docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md.

- **Material layers (M73.6a):** clear coat, sheen, anisotropy and the dielectric specular (`specular`, `ior`) are drawn on every backend
  (OpenGL, GLES3, WebGL2, D3D11, Metal) by the standard mesh shading, image-based light included (approximated: docs/rendering/RENDERING_LIGHTING.md,
  "The material layers"); `MeshRenderer.clearcoat`, `clearcoatRoughness`, `sheenColor`, `sheenRoughness`, `anisotropy`, `anisotropyRotation`,
  `specular` and `ior` no longer say "No effect yet", `render.supports` answers `clearCoat`, `sheen` and `anisotropy` from the GPU, and a custom
  material's `Surface` has the same fields. The defaults draw exactly the pictures of before (ior 1.5 and specular 1 are F0 0.04), so no
  golden image changed. glTF import reads `KHR_materials_clearcoat`, `_sheen`, `_anisotropy`, `_specular` and `_ior`: the factors always, the
  textures packed into free channels of the occlusion and metallic-roughness maps; what does not fit (the clear coat's normal map, the sheen
  and specular colour textures, the direction of the anisotropy texture) is named in a warning with the file, the material and what is
  used instead. Editable meshes keep the factors and write them back to glTF. Every custom material's shader twin was regenerated.

- **LTC tables for area lights (M73, no renderer change yet):** `tools/ltc` fits Linearly Transformed Cosines tables for the engine's own GGX BRDF (deterministic, same bits with any thread count) and writes the committed `engine/render/src/ltc_tables.h`; `engine/render/shaders/ltc.glsl` and a CPU reference integrate polygon lights over them.
