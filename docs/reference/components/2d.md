# Components: 2D

## Bone2D

Since 0.7.0.

A bone of a 2D skeleton, on a plain entity of the hierarchy (docs/ANIMATION.md, "Bones and IK"). The bone starts at the entity's origin and runs `length` units along its local +X axis, so its tip is where a child bone is usually put (position [length, 0]). Bones nest by being children of each other; a clip, a script or IK2D poses a bone through its Transform (rotation.z turns it, position and scale move and stretch it). A SpriteSkin deforms a Sprite's mesh with the bones; the editor draws the bones of the selection, and `debug.bones()` draws them in a game.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `length` | float | `32.0` | How far the bone runs along the entity's local +X axis, from its origin to its tip, in local units (the entity's Transform.scale stretches it). The tip is where the weights of a SpriteSkin and the IK2D solvers measure to. 32 by default. Range 0.0..100000.0. |
| `minAngle` | float | `-180.0` | The lowest Transform.rotation.z (degrees, in the parent's frame) IK2D may give this bone. Only IK2D keeps a bone inside the limits: a clip or a script can pose it anywhere. With maxAngle - minAngle of 360 or more (the default -180 .. 180) the bone is free. Example: a knee that only bends one way is minAngle 0, maxAngle 150. Range -360.0..360.0. |
| `maxAngle` | float | `180.0` | The highest Transform.rotation.z (degrees, in the parent's frame) IK2D may give this bone; see minAngle. Range -360.0..360.0. |

## IK2D

Since 0.7.0.

Inverse kinematics for a chain of Bone2D entities: put it on the LAST bone (the hand, the foot) and name a `target`; every fixed step, after the animators have posed the bones and before the frame is drawn, the bones from this one up through its parents are turned so the tip of this bone reaches the target entity's position, within each bone's minAngle / maxAngle. Feet on the ground, a hand on a lever, a head that looks at something (docs/ANIMATION.md, "Bones and IK"). The solvers are deterministic. A target out of reach leaves the chain straight, pointing at it. With several IK2D they solve in the order their components were added. IK2D rewrites the bones' rotation.z each step: the animator (or you) pose the bones before it, it adjusts them.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `target` | entity |  | Id of the entity to reach: the tip of this bone (its origin plus Bone2D.length along its +X) is brought to the target's world position. Empty = the component does nothing. |
| `chainLength` | int | `2` | ccd: how many bones turn, this one and its parents upward (each must have a Bone2D and be the parent of the one before). twoBone ignores it: it always turns this bone and its parent. 2 by default. Range 1.0..16.0. |
| `bend` | enum: positive \\| negative | `"positive"` | twoBone: which way the middle joint (the elbow, the knee) bends. positive = this bone's rotation.z ends up counter-clockwise (greater) from the one above it, negative = clockwise. A character facing right with a knee in front usually wants one of them; flipping the whole character (a negative Transform.scale.x) is taken into account, so the knee stays in front. |
| `weight` | float | `1.0` | 0 to 1. 1 = the chain takes the solved pose; 0 = the animated pose stays; in between blends each bone's angle the short way round. Fade it in while a foot is planted, out in the air. Range 0.0..1.0. |
| `solver` | enum: twoBone \\| ccd | `"twoBone"` | twoBone: an exact solution for two bones (a leg, an arm), reaches whenever the target is within the two bones' length and honours `bend`. ccd: Cyclic Coordinate Descent, any chain of chainLength bones (a tail, a tentacle, a spine); it converges in at most 24 sweeps, bends where the limits allow and ignores `bend`. |

## Light2D

Since 0.2.0.

A light for lit 2D visuals: sprites, shapes and tilemaps with `lit` true. Their colour is multiplied by SceneSettings.ambientLight plus the light of every Light2D (point: color * intensity * (1 - distance / radius)^falloff, scaled by the normal map's angle to the light when the sprite has a normalMap; directional: even light from one direction). Unlit visuals ignore lights, so existing games look the same. Point lights are added up in a light buffer the size of the picture (SceneSettings.lightScale), so a scene can have hundreds: at most 256 lights shade a frame, directional lights first, then the point lights whose radius reaches the camera view (the ones with more of their radius in view first, then the more intense ones); a light entirely off-view is left out. More than 256 in view logs one warning per scene, and renderStats().lights2d / lights2dDropped count what was kept and left out. A lit sprite or tile WITH a normal map is shaded per pixel by the first 16 of those lights (directional first), the rest only lights flat surfaces. `castShadows` lets the solid tiles of a Tilemap with occludesLight cast shadows (at most 32 shadow-casting lights per frame). The light sits at the entity's position (Transform) and ignores layers.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `type` | enum: point \\| directional | `"point"` | point: a lamp at the entity's position that fades out at `radius`. directional: sun / moon, the same everywhere, coming from the opposite of `direction`. |
| `color` | color | `[1.0,1.0,1.0,1.0]` | Light colour. Accepts [r,g,b,a], "#rrggbb" or a name (alpha is ignored). |
| `intensity` | float | `1.0` | Strength: multiplies color. 1 = full colour at the lamp; above 1 over-brightens. Range 0.0..100.0. |
| `radius` | float | `300.0` | Point lights: distance in world units at which the light has faded to nothing. Range 0.0..100000.0. |
| `falloff` | float | `2.0` | Point lights: fade curve exponent. 1 = linear, 2 (default) = smooth, higher = a tight bright core. Range 0.01..16.0. |
| `height` | float | `80.0` | Point lights: height above the drawing plane in world units. Only matters for normal-mapped sprites: a low lamp grazes the surface and shows relief, a high one lights it flatly. Range 0.0..100000.0. |
| `direction` | float | `270.0` | Directional lights: degrees the light travels towards (0 = right, 90 = up, 270 = down). |
| `elevation` | float | `45.0` | Directional lights: angle above the drawing plane in degrees (90 = straight at the picture, 0 = along it). Range 0.0..90.0. |
| `castShadows` | bool | `false` | Point lights: true = the solid tiles of every Tilemap with occludesLight block this light, so the far side of a wall stays dark. The first solid tile the light reaches is lit (its face); the tiles and floor behind it are in shadow. A light placed inside a solid tile shines out of it. Shadows follow tile edits (set_tile) on the next frame. Costs more than a plain light: see PERFORMANCE.md; at most 32 shadow-casting lights are shaded per frame (the ones ranked first), the others shine through walls. Directional lights cast no shadows. Default false. (since 0.3.0) |

## Parallax

Since 0.2.0.

Shifts the drawing of this entity's world-space visuals (Sprite, Shape2D, Text, Tilemap) with the camera so far layers slide slower than near ones. Offset = camera position * (1 - factor) per axis: factor 1 behaves like any world object, 0 stays glued to the camera (a sky), 0.5 is half as fast (distant hills), above 1 is a foreground. With the camera at (0, 0) the entity is exactly where its Transform says. Only the drawing moves: Transform, physics and picking positions stay put, while entity.bounds() reports where it is drawn. Deterministic: it depends only on the camera position.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `factor` | vec2 | `[0.5,0.5]` | [x, y] fraction of the camera's movement this layer follows (0 = fixed on screen, 1 = a normal world object). |
| `repeatX` | bool | `false` | Sprite only. Tile the sprite endlessly left and right (at `repeatSize.x` spacing, or its own width) so a background strip never runs out. The copies are drawn only where the camera sees them. |
| `repeatY` | bool | `false` | Sprite only. Same as repeatX, up and down. |
| `repeatSize` | vec2 | `[0.0,0.0]` | Distance between repeated copies in world units [x, y]. 0 = the sprite's own width / height. Use a smaller value to overlap soft edges, a larger one for gaps. |
| `offset` | vec2 | `[0.0,0.0]` | Runtime: the shift [x, y] currently applied to the drawing (written by the renderer every frame). Read-only. Runtime-only (not saved). |

## ParticleEmitter

Emits short-lived particles (smoke, sparks, explosions). Particles interpolate size and color over their lifetime.

|---|---|---|---|

| `rate` | float | `20.0` | Particles emitted per second while emitting. Range 0.0..100000.0. |
| `burst` | int | `0` | Number of particles emitted once when the emitter starts (explosions). Range 0.0..100000.0. |
| `maxParticles` | int | `500` | Upper limit of simultaneously alive particles. Range 1.0..1000000.0. |
| `lifetime` | float | `1.0` | How long a particle lives, in seconds. Range 0.001..3600.0. |
| `lifetimeVariance` | float | `0.2` | Random +- fraction applied to lifetime (0.2 = +-20%). Range 0.0..1.0. |
| `speed` | float | `100.0` | Initial speed in world units per second. |
| `speedVariance` | float | `0.3` | Random +- fraction applied to speed. Range 0.0..1.0. |
| `direction` | float | `90.0` | 2D emission direction in degrees: 0 = right, 90 = up. |
| `spread` | float | `30.0` | Half-angle of the emission cone in degrees (180 = all directions). Range 0.0..180.0. |
| `gravity` | vec3 | `[0.0,-200.0,0.0]` | Constant acceleration [x, y, z] in world units/s^2 (default pulls down). |
| `startSize` | float | `8.0` | Particle size when born, in world units. |
| `endSize` | float | `0.0` | Particle size at the end of its life. |
| `startColor` | color | `[1.0,1.0,1.0,1.0]` | Particle color when born. |
| `endColor` | color | `[1.0,1.0,1.0,0.0]` | Particle color at the end of life (alpha 0 fades out). |
| `texture` | asset (texture) | `""` | Particle image. Empty uses a soft round particle. |
| `worldSpace` | bool | `true` | true = particles stay where they were emitted when the emitter moves; false = they follow the emitter. |
| `is3D` | bool | `false` | Emit in a 3D cone as camera-facing billboards instead of in the 2D plane. |
| `layer` | int | `50` | Draw order. Higher layers draw on top. |
| `blend` | enum: alpha \\| additive \\| multiply | `"alpha"` | Compositing of particles: additive is ideal for sparks, fire, magic and neon effects. |
| `autoDestroy` | bool | `false` | Destroy the entity automatically once it is not emitting and all its particles died. Use with burst > 0 and emitting = false for one-shot effects (explosions, pickups). |

Ignored (treated as world) when is3D is true. |
| `anchor` | vec2 | `[0.0,0.0]` | Screen space only: screen point the position is relative to ([0,0] top-left, [1,0] top-right, [0.5,0.5] center, [1,1] bottom-right). |
| `safeArea` | bool | `false` | Screen space only. true: the anchor names a point of the SAFE area, the screen minus the notch, rounded corners and system bars of a phone (screen.safeArea in scripts), instead of the whole screen: anchor [0, 0] is the top-left corner of the safe area, [1, 1] its bottom-right. Keeps HUD and buttons out of the notch. On a screen without cutouts (desktop, most tablets) it changes nothing. Default false. (since 0.2.0) |

## Shape2D

Untextured 2D primitive for prototypes, UI panels and debug visuals.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shape` | enum: rectangle \\| circle \\| line \\| triangle \\| diamond \\| polygon \\| star | `"rectangle"` | Kind of primitive to draw. triangle: isosceles, apex at the middle of the top edge of the size box, base along the bottom edge. diamond: a rhombus whose 4 points sit at the middles of the size box's 4 edges (a square rotated 45 degrees when size is square; handy for gems, enemies and neon markers). polygon: a regular polygon with `sides` sides whose vertices lie on the ellipse inscribed in the size box, first vertex pointing up (hexagons, pentagons, octagons; many sides approach a circle). star: `points` tips on that ellipse, first tip pointing up, with notches at `innerRadius` of the outer radius in between. Outlines (filled = false) of triangle, diamond, polygon and star grow INWARD from the silhouette, like the rectangle's, so a thick outline never pokes out of the size box. |
| `size` | vec2 | `[32.0,32.0]` | rectangle/triangle/diamond/polygon/star: [width, height] of the box that contains the shape. circle: diameter in x. line: end point offset from the entity origin. In screen space (space "screen", +Y down) a line's size.y points UP, unlike the size.y of Panels, Texts and Sprites, which grows downwards: size [100, 40] ends 100 px right and 40 px above the start, [100, -40] ends below it. A chart bar is a rectangle, not a line: give it pivot [0, 1] so size [w, h] hangs w right and h down from its position. |
| `color` | color | `[1.0,1.0,1.0,1.0]` | Fill or outline color. |
| `filled` | bool | `true` | true = solid shape, false = outline only (lines are always drawn as strokes). |
| `thickness` | float | `2.0` | Outline / line thickness in world units. Range 0.0..10000.0. |
| `layer` | int | `0` | Draw order. Higher layers draw on top. |
| `space` | enum: world \\| screen | `"world"` | world: affected by cameras. screen: UI in design pixels, origin TOP-LEFT, +Y down. |
| `pivot` | vec2 | `[0.5,0.5]` | Anchor point within the shape's size box for every shape but line: [0.5,0.5] = the box centre sits on the position (default), [0,0] bottom-left, [1,1] top-right. This point is also what the entity rotates and scales around. In screen space +Y is down, so [0,1] puts the top-left corner at the position. For a triangle the box centre is NOT its centre of mass (the apex is at the top-middle, the base along the bottom): use [0.5, 0.3333] to put its centroid on the position, the natural point to spin a triangle around without it wobbling. Circles and diamonds are symmetric, so the default [0.5,0.5] already spins them in place. |
| `blend` | enum: alpha \\| additive \\| multiply | `"alpha"` | Compositing: alpha (normal), additive (glow/neon), multiply (darken). |
| `anchor` | vec2 | `[0.0,0.0]` | Screen space only: screen point the position is relative to ([0,0] top-left, [1,1] bottom-right). |
| `safeArea` | bool | `false` | Screen space only. true: the anchor names a point of the SAFE area, the screen minus the notch, rounded corners and system bars of a phone (screen.safeArea in scripts), instead of the whole screen: anchor [0, 0] is the top-left corner of the safe area, [1, 1] its bottom-right. Keeps HUD and buttons out of the notch. On a screen without cutouts (desktop, most tablets) it changes nothing. Default false. (since 0.2.0) |
| `sides` | int | `6` | polygon only: number of sides (3 = triangle, 6 = hexagon, 8 = octagon). Range 3.0..64.0. |
| `points` | int | `5` | star only: number of tips (5 = the classic star). Range 3.0..64.0. |
| `innerRadius` | float | `0.5` | star only: radius of the notches between the tips as a fraction of the outer radius. 0.5 = classic star, near 1 = a fat spiky polygon, near 0 = thin needles. Range 0.0..1.0. |
| `lit` | bool | `false` | World space only. true: the shape is shaded by the Light2D entities of the scene plus SceneSettings.ambientLight (its colour is multiplied by ambient + the sum of the lights), like Sprite.lit, so a dark level can use shapes for platforms and hazards; every shape type, outlines and lines included, is flat (no normal map). false (default): drawn exactly in `color`, whatever lights exist. How many lights shade a frame: see Light2D. (since 0.2.0) |
| `cornerRadius` | float | `0.0` | rectangle only: radius of the rounded corners in world units, antialiased (a button, a card, a page). Clamped to half the shorter side, so a large value makes a pill. 0 (default) = square corners, drawn as before. Other shapes ignore it. Range 0.0..100000.0. (since 0.4.0) |
| `softness` | float | `0.0` | rectangle and circle only: width in world units of a blurred edge, centred on the shape's outline (half of it fades in inside the outline, half outside, so the shape's footprint grows by softness / 2 on every side). Turns a shape into a soft drop shadow (a dark translucent rounded rectangle, a little offset, softness 12-30), a vignette or a light pool (a circle with softness near its diameter and an additive blend). Follows cornerRadius; works with filled = false (a blurred ring) and with a gradient. 0 (default) = a crisp, antialiased edge, as before. Triangles, diamonds, polygons, stars and lines ignore it (draw a circle or rectangle for a soft shape). Range 0.0..100000.0. (since 0.4.0) |
| `gradient` | enum: none \\| linear \\| radial | `"none"` | rectangle and circle fill only (filled = true): none (default) = flat `color`. linear = `color` blends to `color2` along the direction gradientAngle, from the box edge to the opposite edge (paper shading, a gutter, a sky). radial = `color` at the centre blends to `color2` at the edge of the shape's box (a vignette, a light pool, a glow); outside the box's inscribed ellipse it stays color2. The blend is computed per pixel (and dithered), so it is smooth with no banding, in one entity. Alpha is blended too (premultiplied, so a transparent color2 fades color out without greying it): make color2 transparent to fade out. A gradient whose `color` is transparent still draws when `color2` is not (the vignette); the shape is skipped only when every colour it uses is fully transparent. Ignored by outlines and by the other shapes. (since 0.4.0) |
| `color2` | color | `[1.0,1.0,1.0,1.0]` | gradient only: the colour at the end of the gradient (the right/top end of a linear one, the edge of a radial one). `color` is the start. Include its alpha: [0, 0, 0, 0] fades `color` to nothing. (since 0.4.0) |
| `gradientAngle` | float | `0.0` | linear gradient only: direction in degrees, measured like a rotation (counter-clockwise, y up in world space): 0 = `color` on the left to `color2` on the right, 90 = `color` at the bottom to `color2` at the top, 270 = top to bottom. The gradient spans the shape's box exactly from corner to corner and turns with the entity. In screen space, where y is down, 90 still goes bottom to top on the screen. (since 0.4.0) |
| `gradientStops` | json | `[]` | gradient only: more than two colours. An array of [position, colour] pairs, position 0..1 along the gradient (0 = where `color` was, 1 = the far end or the edge of a radial one), colours as [r,g,b,a], "#rrggbb" or a name, e.g. [[0, "#000000cc"], [0.6, [0, 0, 0, 0.25]], [1, [0, 0, 0, 0]]] for a soft shadow that dies out slowly. Up to 8 stops (the others are ignored), sorted by position for you; before the first stop and after the last one the gradient keeps that stop's colour. Alpha blends like `color` -> `color2` (premultiplied). With stops, `color` and `color2` are not used for the fill, and the shape is skipped only when every stop is fully transparent. [] (default) = the two-colour gradient `color` -> `color2`. The colours are baked into a 256-step ramp (one row of a shared texture per distinct ramp, at most 256 ramps at once), so the cost is the same as a two-colour gradient, shapes with equal ramps still batch, and a script may change the stops every frame. An entry that is not [number, colour] is skipped with one warning. (since 0.4.0) |
| `gradientEase` | enum: linear \\| smoothstep \\| quadIn \\| quadOut \\| quadInOut \\| cubicIn \\| cubicOut \\| cubicInOut \\| quartIn \\| quartOut \\| quartInOut \\| quintIn \\| quintOut \\| quintInOut \\| sineIn \\| sineOut \\| sineInOut \\| expoIn \\| expoOut \\| expoInOut \\| circIn \\| circOut \\| circInOut | `"linear"` | gradient only: the curve the position follows before the colours are looked up, which removes the visible kink where a linear fade meets its end. The names are those of the script tween easings (docs/ANIMATION.md). linear (default) = even steps. A soft shadow or vignette that fades `color` -> a transparent `color2` looks natural with quadOut/cubicOut (fast start, long gentle tail, no edge) or smoothstep (no kink at either end); quadIn/cubicIn hold the start colour longer. It applies to the two-colour gradient and to gradientStops alike (stop positions are on the curve's input side: a stop at 0.5 is where the eased position is 0.5). The shape costs the same as a linear gradient. (since 0.4.0) |

## Sprite

Draws a textured (or solid color) quad. Use `region` to pick a frame from a sprite sheet. Draw order: higher `layer` on top; ties sort by position.y (lower y on top), then creation order.

|---|---|---|---|
| `texture` | asset (texture) | `""` | Project-relative path of the image (e.g. "assets/hero.png"). Empty draws a solid quad of `color`. A picture made at run time (`render.captureTexture`, `render.textureFromPNG`) works here too: use its `id` ("runtime:3"; docs/SCRIPTING.md, "Render"). So does the live picture of a camera: "target:<name>" for the Camera whose `renderTarget` is that name (docs/RENDERING.md, "Render targets"). |
| `region` | rect | `[0.0,0.0,0.0,0.0]` | Source rectangle [x, y, w, h] in texture pixels with the origin at the TOP-LEFT. A zero size means the whole texture. SpriteAnimator overwrites it every frame. |
| `size` | vec2 | `[0.0,0.0]` | Size [w, h] in world units. [0, 0] = use the region (or texture) size in pixels. |
| `pivot` | vec2 | `[0.5,0.5]` | Anchor inside the sprite: [0, 0] bottom-left, [0.5, 0.5] center, [1, 1] top-right. |
| `color` | color | `[1.0,1.0,1.0,1.0]` | Tint multiplied with the texture. Alpha makes it translucent. Accepts [r,g,b,a], "#rrggbb" or a name. |
| `flipX` | bool | `false` | Mirror horizontally. |
| `flipY` | bool | `false` | Mirror vertically. |
| `layer` | int | `0` | Draw order. Higher layers draw on top of lower ones. Backgrounds use negative layers. |
| `space` | enum: world \\| screen | `"world"` | world: positioned in the game world and affected by cameras. screen: UI/HUD in design-resolution pixels with (0,0) at the TOP-LEFT and +Y DOWN; drawn after the world. |
| `pixelArt` | bool | `true` | true = nearest-neighbour filtering (crisp pixels), false = smooth linear filtering. |
| `blend` | enum: alpha \\| additive \\| multiply | `"alpha"` | Compositing: alpha (normal), additive (glow, neon, fire, lasers; brightens), multiply (darkens/tints). |
| `anchor` | vec2 | `[0.0,0.0]` | Screen space only: screen point the position is relative to. [0,0] top-left (default), [1,0] top-right, [0.5,0.5] center, [1,1] bottom-right. Keeps HUD elements glued to screen edges on any aspect ratio. |

[0, 0, 0, 0] (default) stretches the whole region. Borders that do not fit in `size` shrink together. Works with atlas-packed textures, flipX / flipY and a lit sprite. Screen-space UI panels use the same slicing through Panel.texture and Panel.slice. (since 0.2.0) |
| `lit` | bool | `false` | World space only. true: the sprite is shaded by the Light2D entities of the scene plus SceneSettings.ambientLight (its colour is multiplied by ambient + the sum of the lights); false (default): drawn exactly as the texture says, whatever lights exist. How many lights shade a frame: see Light2D. (since 0.2.0) |

It turns with the sprite's rotation and flips. Empty (default) = a flat sprite lit only by distance. Ignored unless `lit` is true. (since 0.2.0) |
| `skew` | vec2 | `[0.0,0.0]` | Shear in degrees [x, y] about the pivot, applied before the entity's rotation and scale: x = 20 leans the top edge to the right (an italic slant, a sign swinging in wind, a page seen at an angle), x = -20 to the left; y = 20 tilts the right edge up (a flag in a gust). | `meshRows` | int | `0` | Grid cells down for a deformable sprite (see meshColumns). 0 (default) = a plain quad. Range 0.0..128.0. (since 0.4.0) |
| `vertexOffsets` | json | `[]` | Deformable sprites only (meshColumns > 0 and meshRows > 0): how far each grid vertex is moved from its place, in the sprite's local units (the size it is drawn at, before scale and rotation; +x right, +y UP). | `vertexColors` | json | `[]` | Deformable sprites only (meshColumns > 0 and meshRows > 0): a colour for every grid vertex, multiplied with `color` (and Tint), blended smoothly across each cell. A flat array of numbers [r0, g0, b0, a0, r1, g1, b1, a1, ...], components 0..1, one RGBA per vertex in the order of `vertexOffsets` (row by row from the TOP row, left to right): vertex (column c, row r) starts at index (r * (meshColumns + 1) + c) * 4. The length must be exactly (meshColumns + 1) * (meshRows + 1) * 4: a scene or prefab with another length is an error naming the length it needs, and a script that writes a wrong length gets one warning and the colours are ignored. A bent page gets its light and dark along the curve with [0.6,0.6,0.6,1] at the vertices in shade and [1,1,1,1] in light. Write the whole array to change it (like vertexOffsets). [] (default) = every vertex white. Not used with SpriteSkin. (since 0.9.0) |

## SpriteAnimator

Flipbook animation over a grid sprite sheet. Requires a Sprite on the same entity; it overwrites Sprite.region every frame. Frames are numbered row-major from the top-left. Set `clip` to play a named clip of the texture's `sheet` entry in assets.json (grid, frames, fps, loop and pivot come from there; `assets.animate_svg`, `assets.bake_sheet`, `assets.slice_sheet` and the Aseprite import write it): a scene then names only the texture and the clip. The numeric fields override the clip when they differ from their default (columns 1, rows 1, firstFrame 0, lastFrame 0, fps 10, loop true). From a script: entity.spriteAnimator.play(name). A non-looping clip that reaches its end sends the game event `clipEnded` {id, clip}.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `columns` | int | `1` | Number of columns in the sprite sheet grid. With `clip`, set it (not 1) only to override the sheet entry. Range 1.0..4096.0. |
| `rows` | int | `1` | Number of rows in the sprite sheet grid. Range 1.0..4096.0. |
| `firstFrame` | int | `0` | First frame of the clip (inclusive, row-major index). |
| `lastFrame` | int | `0` | Last frame of the clip (inclusive, row-major index). |
| `fps` | float | `10.0` | Playback speed in frames per second. Range 0.0..1000.0. |
| `loop` | bool | `true` | Restart from firstFrame after lastFrame. If false the animation stops on the last frame and sends `clipEnded`. With `clip`, false overrides a looping clip; true (the default) leaves the clip's own loop. |
| `playing` | bool | `true` | Set false to pause, true to play. |
| `clip` | string | `""` | Name of a clip of the Sprite texture's `sheet` entry in assets.json ("run"); "" = none, the numeric fields alone. Changing it restarts the animation. A name the sheet does not have is an error that lists the clips ("did you mean"): `factoria validate` finds it in scenes, a script's play(name) throws it, a running game logs it once and shows the first frame. (since 0.6.0) |
| `mode` | enum: forward \\| reverse \\| pingpong | `"forward"` | Order of the frames: forward (first to last), reverse (last to first) or pingpong (first to last and back, without repeating the end frames). A pingpong clip that does not loop stops back on the first frame. (since 0.6.0) |
| `sheetPivot` | bool | `true` | true = the pivot recorded in the sheet entry (or the clip's own pivot) is used as Sprite.pivot while the Sprite's own pivot is the default centre [0.5, 0.5]; a Sprite that sets another pivot keeps it. | `ended` | bool | `false` | Runtime state: a non-looping clip reached its end (clipEnded was sent). Runtime-only (not saved). (since 0.6.0) |
| `playedClip` | string | `""` | Runtime state: the clip `time` counts in. Runtime-only (not saved). (since 0.6.0) |
| `time` | float | `0.0` | Runtime state: seconds elapsed in the current clip. Runtime-only (not saved). |
| `frame` | int | `0` | Runtime state: current frame index. Runtime-only (not saved). |

## SpriteSkin

Since 0.7.0.

Deforms the Sprite on the same entity with bones (docs/ANIMATION.md, "Bones and IK"): each vertex of the sprite's 2D mesh follows up to 4 bones, so one picture bends at an elbow or a tail without cutting it in pieces. The mesh is the Sprite's meshColumns x meshRows grid, or with `outline` a grid cut to the texture's opaque pixels. The skin remembers where the bones were when it was bound (`bindPose`: the first time the engine sees it, so pose the bones at rest in the scene; write `bindPose` as [] to bind again at the current pose). A bone that moves from its bind pose drags its vertices with it; at the bind pose the sprite looks exactly as without a skin. It is deformed on the CPU into the sprite's own batch: no extra draw call and no shader. World-space sprites only. Needs Sprite.meshColumns / meshRows or `outline`; Sprite.vertexOffsets still apply (before the bones) and Sprite.skew after them.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bones` | json | `[]` | The bones that move this sprite: an array of strings, each the id of a Bone2D entity ("hip") or the name of one, or a path of names ("skeleton/arm/hand"). A name or path is looked up under the sprite's entity, then under its parent, then under the grandparent and so on, so a character prefab can be instanced many times; an id is looked up in the whole scene. The order is the bone index of `weights` and `bindPose`. At most 64 bones. A name that finds no Bone2D is logged once, with a "did you mean", and that bone is left out. [] = no skin. |
| `weights` | json | `"auto"` | How the vertices follow the bones. "auto" (default): each vertex follows the nearest bones (at most 4), weighted by 1 / (distance to the bone's segment)^2 from the bind pose, so a vertex on a bone follows it alone and one between two joints blends them; it is the same every run. Or an array with one entry per grid vertex, in the order of Sprite.vertexOffsets (row by row from the TOP row, left to right; with `outline` the full grid, vertices the outline leaves out are skipped): an entry is [bone, weight, bone, weight, ...] with up to 4 pairs, `bone` an index into `bones`, weights normalised to 1; a missing or empty entry leaves the vertex where it is. |
| `bindPose` | json | `[]` | Where each bone was when the skin was bound, relative to the sprite: 6 numbers [a, b, c, d, tx, ty] per bone (the bone's 2D matrix in the sprite's local space). The engine writes it the first time it sees the skin; [] binds again, at the pose the bones have then. If its length does not match `bones` it is recorded again. |
| `outline` | bool | `false` | true: instead of Sprite.meshColumns / meshRows, the mesh is a grid of outlineDetail cells along the sprite's longer side, keeping only the cells that hold opaque pixels (alpha above 5%) of the texture's region, so a thin limb or a tail costs vertices where it is and transparent corners cost nothing. Needs the texture as an image file in the project (png, jpg, tga, bmp): a texture an export packed into an atlas, a `.ktx2` only or a picture made at run time draws the full grid instead, with one warning. Hot reload of the texture retraces it. |
| `outlineDetail` | int | `16` | outline only: how many cells the grid has along the sprite's longer side (the shorter side gets as many as keep the cells square). More cells follow the shape closer and deform smoother, at one quad per kept cell. 16 by default. Range 2.0..64.0. |

## Tint

Since 0.4.0.

Multiplies a colour (and an opacity) into every 2D visual of this entity and of all its children, grandchildren and so on: Sprite, Shape2D, Text (glyph colours and rich-text colours too), Tilemap, ParticleEmitter, and the screen-space UI widgets (Panel, Button, Toggle, Slider, TextField, ScrollList, the touch controls, Video, CameraView, Chart, Captions) drawn by the renderer. Put it on the root of a group to fade it out (color [1, 1, 1, 0.5]), turn it red (a damage flash), or darken a whole room, without touching each visual. The tints of an entity and all its ancestors multiply together, so nested groups compose; alpha is multiplied too (a 0.5 inside a 0.5 shows at 0.25), and a visual whose resulting opacity is 0 is not drawn. It multiplies the colours the visual already has (Sprite.color, Shape2D.color and color2 and every gradient stop, Text.color, Tilemap.color, a particle's own colour over its life, Panel.color) and does not affect 3D MeshRenderer, lights, the debug draw or the camera. Entities without a Tint ancestor cost nothing extra: the renderer only walks the parents when at least one Tint exists in the scene.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `color` | color | `[1.0,1.0,1.0,1.0]` | RGBA multiplied into the colour of the visuals of this entity and its descendants. [1, 1, 1, 1] (default) changes nothing. Alpha is a group opacity: [1, 1, 1, 0.5] draws the group at half opacity (each visual is faded on its own, so overlapping visuals of the group still show through one another). Accepts [r,g,b,a], "#rrggbb" or a name. |
