# FactorIA architecture: area lights, the LTC tables (M73)

Part of [FactorIA architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md) (the entry page lists every page). This page is the foundation of area-light
shading: the tables of Linearly Transformed Cosines (LTC) fitted for the engine's own BRDF, the library that fits them, the CPU
reference of the polygon integral and the GLSL that will do the same in a fragment shader. Nothing in the renderer reads the tables
yet (the light component, the textures and the shader hook come in later packages); `engine/render/shaders/ltc.glsl` is in no
template, so no shader hash changes.

## The method

Linearly Transformed Cosines (Heitz, Dupuy, Hill and Neubelt, "Real-Time Polygonal-Light Shading with Linearly Transformed
Cosines", SIGGRAPH 2016) write the lobe of a BRDF times its cosine as a clamped cosine bent by a 3x3 matrix M:
`D(w) = D_o(M^-1 w / |M^-1 w|) |det M^-1| / |M^-1 w|^3`, `D_o(w) = max(0, w.z) / pi`. The integral of D over a polygon is the form
factor of the polygon after M^-1 (a closed form, one arc cosine per edge), so a polygonal light costs about as much as a few
point lights whatever its size. Only the method comes from the paper; the code, the fit and every number in the tables are ours.

The BRDF is the specular term of `mesh_common.glsl`: GGX distribution, Smith height-correlated visibility
(`0.5 / mix(2 nl nv, nl + nv, alpha)`), Schlick Fresnel, with `alpha = roughness^2` for the perceptual roughness the shader uses
(clamped to 0.05..1). The frame of a table cell has `N = +z` and `V = (sin t, 0, cos t)`; the BRDF is symmetric about the xz
plane, so `M^-1 = [[a 0 b] [0 1 0] [c 0 d]]` has four numbers (the scale is free and fixed by the 1 in the middle).

## What a table holds

`engine/render/src/ltc_tables.h` (namespace `fa::ltc_tables`, `// Generated by tools/ltc ... do not edit`) holds two float arrays
of 64 x 64 cells, row `j` = view axis, cell `i` = roughness axis, cell index `j * 64 + i`:

- `kMatrix` (4 floats per cell): `a, b, c, d` of M^-1.
- `kBrdf` (2 floats per cell): `magnitude`, the integral of `f cos` over the hemisphere with F = 1 (the directional albedo of
  the specular lobe without Fresnel), and `fresnel`, the same integral weighted by `(1 - V.H)^5`.

Axes: `u = (roughness - 0.05) / 0.95` and `v = sqrt(1 - N.V)`, texel centres at `(i + 0.5) / 64`, bilinear filtering. The last view
cell is fitted at `N.V = 0.01`, not at 0 (`kMinCosTheta`). `kHash` is `fa::ltc::table_hash` of both arrays.

Convention for the shader: `radiance = lightColor * (f0 * magnitude + (1 - f0) * fresnel) * integral of D over the light * pi`. No
division by pi anywhere else: the engine's light colours are not divided by pi, and the factor pi is the one point lights carry
(`f` in `brdf_cos` is `D V` without it). The Schlick split `f0 * A + (1 - f0) * B` is exact: `F = f0 + (1 - f0)(1 - V.H)^5`.

## The fit (`tools/ltc`, module `fa_ltc`, depends on `fa_core` only)

- **Per cell:** Nelder-Mead over four parameters (the angle of the lobe's centre away from the BRDF's mean direction, the log
  widths along x and y, the skew), started from the neighbouring cell along the view axis (the first view cell of each roughness, `N.V = 1`, from a sequential pass, roughest first), two
  runs of at most 220 iterations (the second with smaller steps). The error is the cubic error of the paper, `|D - f cos /
  magnitude|^3`, estimated with 768 samples of each density: GGX visible-normal samples of the BRDF and cosine samples bent by the
  lobe, each divided by the sum of the two densities (the balance heuristic), on a Hammersley sequence.
- **Albedo:** `magnitude` and `fresnel` come from 8192 visible-normal samples per cell; the tests check them against
  `albedo_quadrature`, a grid over the half-vector domain that shares no code with the sampler.
- **Determinism:** no `rand()`, no clock, fixed iteration counts, ties broken by the position in the simplex. `sin`, `cos`, `exp`,
  `log` and `atan2` are the library's own (`det_*`, only `+ - * /` and `sqrt`), the repo compiles with `-ffp-contract=off` and
  `/fp:precise`, so the table has the same bits on every platform. The rows run in parallel with `fa::jobs`; each cell is written
  by its own row and nothing is reduced across threads, so one thread and many give the same bits (a test fits a reduced table
  inline and with three workers and compares the hash).
- **The polynomial:** `acos(y) / sqrt(1 - y^2)`, which the edge integral needs, is a degree-6 polynomial (our own Chebyshev
  interpolation, 5e-6 from the function) in both `ltc_polygon.cpp` and `ltc.glsl`.

## Quality, and where the family stops

The error is the L1 distance between the lobe and the BRDF lobe (0 identical, 2 disjoint). Over the table the mean is 0.088; for
`N.V >= 0.3` it stays below 0.3 (below 0.16 for `N.V >= 0.5`, below 0.05 for most cells); a rectangle light across 9 cases (roughness 0.12 to 1, `N.V`
0.3 to 0.95) differs from the brute force integral of the BRDF by 2.2 % at most. Close to grazing (`N.V < 0.15`) the horizon cuts the
lobe in a way a clamped cosine cannot follow: the error there is 0.4 to 0.8 (worst 0.84 at roughness cell 16, view cell 63), more
iterations or samples do not change it, and it is the limit of the family, not of the fit. The magnitude (the energy) stays right
there; the shape is rounder than the true lobe.

## Regenerating the tables

`tools/ltc/generate_ltc.sh` (or the CMake target `fa_ltc_regenerate`) builds `fa_ltc_gen` in Release and rewrites the header
(about 10 seconds on 10 cores). Run it after changing the fit or the BRDF of `mesh_common.glsl`, and commit the result. `fa_ltc_gen
--check <header>` compares a header with the fit; `--size`, `--samples`, `--iterations` give a reduced fit (the tests use 6 cells), and
`--verbose` prints the error grid. The header is in `.clang-format-ignore` and has LF line ends (`.gitattributes`). The tables are
our own data, produced by this code from our own BRDF; no third-party table, code or asset is used or shipped.

## Tests

`fa_test_ltc` (ctest `ltc`): the `det_*` math against the C library; the albedo sampler against the quadrature; the determinism of a
reduced fit (no pool, three workers, a second run); the committed table (hash, finite, positive determinant, smooth between
neighbours, magnitude and Fresnel against the quadrature, lobe error and hemisphere albedo for cells away from grazing); the polygon
integral (polynomial against exact, winding sign, the identity lobe against a brute force form factor, a quad across the horizon); a
rectangle light through the table against a brute force integral of the BRDF. `ltc_tables_up_to_date` (label `slow`, DISABLED in a
Debug build, `ctest -L slow`) fits the whole table again and compares it with the committed header byte for byte.

`engine/render/shaders/ltc.glsl` (`@block fa_ltc`: `fa_ltc_uv`, `fa_ltc_tangent`, `fa_ltc_local`, `fa_ltc_quad`, `fa_ltc_triangle`,
`fa_ltc_rect`) is the same algorithm as `polygon_integral` with a streaming horizon clip (no arrays). It is validated with
sokol-shdc for every backend; it does not run in a render yet.
